# Open questions

Every question waiting on the designer's answer, and only those: there are no others in the other documents.
When one is answered, the answer goes where it belongs (the rules, the specification, the roadmap, the code),
marked *Stated* there, and the question here is cut down to whatever is still open, or removed.

Choices marked *Proposed* elsewhere are decisions made without an answer; they stand as they are and need none.
They are marked only so that it is known which choices were the designer's and which were not, should one ever
need to change. None is a question in disguise: a choice that cannot stand without the designer's answer is
asked here instead (checked, every *Proposed* in the documents, on 2026-11-06).
Anything to be built is in the [backlog](backlog.md) or the [roadmap](grand/roadmap.md), and anything left to
playtesting is marked so where it is written.

## OQ10 A bot moving a person's pieces

With its Raja on a person's throne, an allied bot commands the person's army, and moves its pieces as it judges
best: on 2026-11-12 Green moved the user's Rook off guarding the user's Pawn to take a Boat, and the Pawn was lost.
The bots now count a Chaturaji 2027 Pawn as worth keeping as much as a Horse, so that one would no longer happen
(*Proposed*). But should a bot move a person's pieces at all, or leave them to their player, moving only its own
and those of seats bots play? Or move them only to save one, or to take something left unguarded? The bots know
which seats people play, so any of these can be built.

## OQ9 A winning streak

Should the leaderboard show a winning streak, the games won since the player last lost? Easily raised by playing
weak bots, it could count only the games that count toward Elo (no guest, the players the same throughout, no
player at several seats), or only games against people. Raised by the user on 2026-11-07 as perhaps not worth it:
not built until asked for.

the rest of the game is playable ([specification Q5](grand/specification.md#9-open-questions)).
